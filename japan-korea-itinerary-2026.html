Before you book anything

Two document issues are specific to a Brazilian passport in late 2026. Clear these first — they gate the whole trip.

✅

Japan visa-free — renewed, and it now covers you

Re-checked Aug 2, 2026 — this is resolved. The Brazil–Japan reciprocal 90-day waiver was due to lapse on Sep 29/30, 2026, six weeks before you fly. On Jul 29, 2026 the two governments exchanged notes and extended it by three years, to Sep 29, 2029 — confirmed by Itamaraty and Japan's MOFA. Your Nov 10 – Dec 4 window sits comfortably inside it. No tourist visa needed.

  • You still need an ICAO-compliant ordinary biometric passport (ePassport) — the waiver does not cover non-electronic passports.
  • Covers tourism, business meetings, events and family visits for up to 90 days; no paid work of any kind.
  • Reciprocal, so it renews on the Japanese side too. Nothing to apply for — just show the ePassport at immigration.
  • Only remaining action: check both passports are valid 6+ months beyond Dec 4, 2026.
🇰🇷

Korea needs a K-ETA (Brazil is not exempt)

Brazilians are visa-free for Korea (90 days) but must hold a K-ETA — Brazil is not on the 22-country exemption list.

  • Apply at k-eta.go.kr — ~₩10,000 (≈ US$8), valid 3 years.
  • Processing takes up to 72h — do it at least a week before Nov 23.

The jet lag, handled

São Paulo (UTC−3) → Tokyo (UTC+9) is a 12-hour eastward jump — the harder direction, because your body clock resists advancing. Unaided it can take ~10 days; with light + melatonin discipline you'll feel largely normal by Day 3. That's why the first three days are deliberately gentle.

Your #1 tool

☀️ Chase morning light

You'll wake at 4–5am the first days. Get outside within an hour of waking, before 10:00, every day for 4 days. After 20:00, dim everything.

0.5–1 mg

💉 Melatonin, low + early

Take 0.5–1 mg ~30 min before bedtime (22:00–23:00 JST), from arrival night, 3–5 nights. Never in daytime.

☕ Caffeine & meals

Eat on Tokyo time from landing. Coffee mornings only; hard stop at 2pm. No alcohol first 2–3 days. Hydrate hard.

How these numbers were built

📏 The method, so you can adjust it

Each day is the sum of its legs at realistic door-to-door times, not timetable minutes: platform and transfer time is in, waiting for a Hokkaido bus in winter is in, and airport legs assume 90 min domestic / 2 h international at check-in plus 30–60 min to clear arrivals and bags. Walking between places counts; walking within a park, museum or trail doesn't. Figures are for the itinerary as written — swap the DMZ in on Nov 26, or commute to Disney from Shinjuku, and the totals move by hours. Treat them as planning envelopes ±20%, and pad anything with a snow forecast attached.

🧾

Tax-free changes nine days before you land — Nov 1, 2026

Japan switches from discount-at-the-register to pay-now, refund-at-departure. Every "10% off at the counter" tip you've read online is out of date for your dates.

  • In the shop: show your passport, staff register the sale — but you pay the full tax-inclusive price. Still ¥5,000+ per shop per day; the general-goods/consumables split and the sealed-bag rule are gone.
  • At Narita, Dec 4: customs verifies your passport against the purchase records and can ask to see the goods — so the camera and every receipt fly in your carry-on, never checked. Budget +30–45 min at the airport.
  • The money comes back later from the shop or its refund agent — card refunds ~1–2 weeks, bank transfer 2–4. So a ¥200,000 camera means ¥20,000 floating on your card for a fortnight; size the trip's cash buffer accordingly.
  • You must leave Japan within 90 days of the purchase — never an issue here (24 days max).
  • Used gear qualifies if the shop is a registered tax-free store — Map, Kitamura, Yodobashi, Sofmap and Bic all are; the back-street vintage places usually aren't. Ask before you commit.

Read the grade

🏷 AB / B / C · and ジャンク

Japanese shops grade used gear on a letter scale (roughly AB = near-mint, B = light wear, C = well used). ジャンク (junk) means sold as-is, no returns, no warranty — often a great lens with fungus, sometimes a brick. Map & Kitamura back their used stock with their own warranty (typically ~6 months) — confirm at the counter.

Before you pay

🔍 The 3-minute check

Ask to mount and fire it: shutter count (Sony/Nikon menus or ask staff), fungus & haze (hold the lens to a light), sensor dust (f/16 shot of a white wall), and every aperture blade. Then check the menu language — some Japan-market bodies ship Japanese-only, and older ones can't be switched.

Bringing it home

🔌 Voltage, warranty, customs

Japan is 100 V, plug type A — camera chargers are almost always 100–240 V, but check laptops/appliances before you buy. Japanese domestic warranties usually don't cover Brazil. And keep the total in mind: Brazil's duty-free allowance for air arrivals is US$1,000 — over that, you declare and pay 50% on the excess.

🎟 Tickets + hotel separately vs. a Disney Vacation Package

Short answer: booking it yourself is far cheaper — and the official packages don't even fit these dates cleanly. A Vacation Package is sold as 2-day = 1 night or 3-day = 2 nights, always at a Disney hotel. It's too big for one visit, so go in with a route.
